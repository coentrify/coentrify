# 0.0.4

Every standard has a Why now, kept to its essence: 35 got the one they lacked. Nothing in it is stable yet.

- 196 standards in 21 modules: 52 changed and six came in
- A sweep over every Why, each a goal or a principle that is written actively: the Read First standards are done
- Harmony speaks of fine-tuning a balance, where it said to decide for the case at hand
- A standard on filing a change with coentrify, for a project that is a contributor
- Always Make No Mistakes as a self-healing feedback loop, in the readme and on every page that names it
- A gate is the whole test of a phase: its leading question and everything it takes to answer it
- A glossary of the terms coentrify uses most, and a standard for the glossary of a project
- Two generic standards: a name describes its unit, and things of one kind are consistent
- A standard on the system behind a mock: a spec test of its own, or a spec and recorded manual tests where no test can run
- The maturity levels of a workspace stand in a standard of their own
- A summary of a linked page stays at the focus level of the page it stands on
- The template asks for the goal or the principle behind a rule as its Why, in a line or two
- The Why of a part page stands on its parent, and the check leaves the part out
- Five docs standards speak of a reader, where a PM, QA or a new joiner stood
- The English variant is a choice, and the recommendations say so
- The commit check passes a standard that is back at its released text
- The changelog has a page per version, and an overview that lists them
