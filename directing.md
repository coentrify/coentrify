# Directing

How you, the director, work with coentrify. The agents follow the standards. This page gathers what the standards ask of you, and the craft of using it.

- The Role - what stays yours
- Your Words - the moves that steer the work
- Your Inboxes - where your time goes, in order

- The Rhythm - a session, a version, a project
- Failure Modes - what goes wrong on the human side

## The Role

You decide what is built, the agents build it, and the checks keep it honest. In the work you specify, plan and accept. Around the work you steer, own and provide ([How It Works](how-it-works.md#the-director)). Everything between your words and a landed, provable change is the agents' job, so your time goes to judgment, taste and creation.

## Your Words

- A go - your approval to build ready work: `FEAT-214 go`, `STORY-8 go`, or one word for all that is ready. An ask is no go. Once work has its go, it is built and lands without asking again.
- A verdict - you accept a story by using it through on main, with its Done parts beside it: `STORY-8 accepted`. Findings go in the chat as they come, become rework in the story, and the story's go starts the rework.
- A veto - an agent names every choice it made on its own. Say which one you want undone. A choice you leave stands.
- A maybe - say an idea with doubt, and the agent looks into it at once and reports what it found.
- A name for the work - call it a prototype, a spike or a workshop before it starts. Unnamed, work takes the full path.
- Short words - "not now" means later, "next is X" names the next topic only, and a short line is read as said, never widened.
- One push back - an agent questions a call once, then takes it.

## Your Inboxes

In the tickets workflow your work comes down to three lists, taken right to left, so you finish before you start:

1. Accept - stories that landed, with proof you can open.
2. Planning - stories ready for a go.
3. Refine - a question only you can answer, as one decision in a plain sentence with a recommendation.

In the simple workflow you ask one question, what's next, and pick from the recommendation. Beside the lists stand your director tasks, the follow-ups only you can do, and the healing log, where each change to the harness waits for your ruling: keep it, change it or revert it.

## What You Hand Over

`coentrify.json` holds your standing decisions ([coentrify.json](coentrify-json.md)).

- `healing` on `automatic` - a caught mistake strengthens the harness at once, and the report names the change for your veto.
- `update` on `automatic` - a newer coentrify is applied when a session starts, and only what needs judgment comes to you.
- `contributor` and `filing` - whether what your project learns goes back to coentrify, and whether you look at each issue first.
- `make no mistakes` - a set up where the agent makes every choice and you veto afterward.

Each setting trades your attention for speed. Start from the defaults, and turn a setting back to `review` when its reports surprise you.

## Your Hats

Next to the agents, you are the rest of the team. You wear the product owner's hat when you choose what comes next, the designer's when you decide how it looks and feels, and the tech lead's when you judge a trade-off an agent puts in front of you. Saying which hat you wear helps: "as the designer, the red goes". An agent can argue from each hat's view, and the decision stays yours.

## Tightening And Loosening

- A Veto line that keeps coming back is a pattern. It becomes a rule, so the Veto empties over time.
- A rule that only adds friction and catches nothing is loosened, with your yes.
- A mistake that comes back means its rule was too weak. The harness heals, and you rule on the change.
- An existing project starts stricter than it is. New work meets the standards from the first day, and old parts are brought up when the work touches them.
- A standard you change to fit turns customized. An update then shows you its changes before anything is replaced.

## The Rhythm

- A session - open the accept list first, plan enough work to keep the builders busy, then refine and brainstorm. The day closes on your word.
- A version - it closes on your word. Feedback on an open version is a cutoff and adds no scope.
- A project - phases, each with one goal and a gate. The gate is a question you answer, and your answer sends the project on or back.

## Failure Modes

- Over-steering - deciding what an agent can settle and you can veto. A choice a small edit undoes is the agent's to make.
- Under-steering - leaving the vision, the pillars or the laws unsaid. The agents then fill the gaps with the average.
- Accepting from the chat - the proof lives in the Done parts and the wiki. Use the product before you accept it.
- A go out of habit - a go covers what it names, and an ask is no go.
- Fighting the structure - when a rule chafes, change the rule. Working around it teaches the agents nothing.
- An unread Veto - the veto is yours only while you read the list.
