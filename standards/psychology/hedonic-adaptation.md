# Hedonic Adaptation

People get used to what is good and what is bad, and the feeling it gives fades. The fading is often incomplete, and a break restores the pleasure.

## Why

The thrill of every upgrade fades, so a game keeps the steps coming and ends before its fun has flattened.

## The Evidence

- Brickman and Campbell 1971, Hedonic relativism and planning the good society, in Appley (ed.), Adaptation-Level Theory, Academic Press, 287-302.
- Frederick and Loewenstein 1999, Hedonic adaptation, in Kahneman, Diener and Schwarz (eds.), Well-Being, Russell Sage Foundation, 302-329.
- Diener, Lucas and Scollon 2006, Beyond the hedonic treadmill, American Psychologist 61(4), 305-314, [doi:10.1037/0003-066X.61.4.305](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.61.4.305). Adaptation is real and often incomplete.
- Nelson and Meyvis 2008, Interrupted consumption: disrupting adaptation to hedonic experiences, Journal of Marketing Research 45(6), 654-664, [doi:10.1509/jmkr.45.6.654](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.45.6.654). A break made a pleasant experience more enjoyable.
- How solid: solid, from life events more than from play.

## Limits

- That small, regular steps beat large, rare ones is plausible and untested.

## The Laws On It

- [Earned Progress](../gamedev/game-design-laws-progress.md#earned-progress) - small, regular steps felt in play
- [As Long As It Is Interesting](../gamedev/game-design-laws-progress.md#as-long-as-it-is-interesting) - the game ends before its fun flattens
- [Easy Pause](../gamedev/game-design-laws-experience.md#easy-pause) and [Natural Breaks](../gamedev/game-design-laws-experience.md#natural-breaks) - a break restores the pleasure
- [Speedrun Ready](../gamedev/game-design-laws-experience.md#speedrun-ready) and [Once Per Save](../gamedev/game-design-laws-experience.md#once-per-save) - a repeat loses its pull, so a returning player goes past what they have seen
- [Cadence](../gamedev/game-design-laws-progress.md#cadence) - each reward comes bigger, or feels bigger, to land the same
