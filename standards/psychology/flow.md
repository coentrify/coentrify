# Flow

Flow is deep, effortless absorption in an activity. It is likeliest with clear goals, immediate feedback, and a challenge that stretches skill without overwhelming it.

## Why

Absorption is what a player comes back for, and its conditions are the part a game controls.

## The Evidence

- Csikszentmihalyi 1990, Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience, Harper and Row. The concept itself dates from Csikszentmihalyi 1975, Beyond Boredom and Anxiety.
- Fong, Zaleski and Leach 2015, The challenge-skill balance and antecedents of flow: a meta-analytic investigation, The Journal of Positive Psychology 10(5), 425-446, [doi:10.1080/17439760.2014.967799](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2014.967799). The balance predicts flow moderately, beside clear goals and a sense of control.
- Cutting, Deterding, Demediuk and Sephton 2023, Difficulty-skill balance does not affect engagement and enjoyment, Royal Society Open Science 10(2), [doi:10.1098/rsos.220274](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.220274). In a pre-registered game study, the balance alone changed neither.
- How solid: solid as a state. The balance is a heuristic.

## Limits

- The balance is one predictor among several, and alone it changed nothing in the one controlled game study.
- A stretch with nothing to do takes away flow's first condition, a clear goal, and absorption breaks. A technical delay is another matter, covered by [Response Time Limits](response-time-limits.md).
