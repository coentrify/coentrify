# Avatar Identification

A player who identifies with the character, the player's own or an ideal self, enjoys the game more and plays it with more intrinsic motivation. Making the character oneself feeds that identification.

## Why

A character the player can recognize as themselves becomes the player's own, and the play with it.

## The Evidence

- Birk, Atkins, Bowey and Mandryk 2016, Fostering intrinsic motivation through avatar identification in digital games, CHI, 2982-2995, [doi:10.1145/2858036.2858062](https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858062). Customizing the avatar raised identification, and with it intrinsic motivation.
- Hefner, Klimmt and Vorderer 2007, Identification with the player character as determinant of video game enjoyment, Entertainment Computing, Lecture Notes in Computer Science, 39-48, [doi:10.1007/978-3-540-74873-1_6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-74873-1_6).
- Yee and Bailenson 2007, The Proteus effect, Human Communication Research 33(3), 271-290, [doi:10.1111/j.1468-2958.2007.00299.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2007.00299.x), and Ratan, Beyea, Li and Graciano 2020, Media Psychology 23(5), 651-675, [doi:10.1080/15213269.2019.1623698](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2019.1623698). A different effect: an avatar's looks shift how the player behaves, with small to medium effects.
- How solid: solid for identification. The Proteus effect is small but steady.

## Limits

- The Proteus effect runs the other way: the avatar changes the player. It supports caution with a character given to the player, never the value of making oneself.
