# Reference Dependence

People judge size, intensity and value against a reference, rarely in absolute terms. The reference drifts toward recent experience and expectations.

## Why

What the player sees means something only against what stands beside it, or what came before it.

## The Evidence

- Helson 1964, Adaptation-Level Theory, Harper and Row. Perception judged against a level that drifts with experience.
- Kahneman and Tversky 1979, Prospect theory: an analysis of decision under risk, Econometrica 47(2), 263-291, [doi:10.2307/1914185](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185). Outcomes valued as gains or losses from a reference point.
- Ruggeri et al. 2020, Replicating patterns of prospect theory for decision under risk, Nature Human Behaviour 4(6), 622-633, [doi:10.1038/s41562-020-0886-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-0886-x). It replicated across 19 countries.
- How solid: solid.

## Limits

- The reference moves. Steady growth turns into the new normal, and the start keeps its pull only while the game keeps it in view, with a revisit or a before and after.
- Contrast sharpens an impression and leaves the absolute level counting too.

- [Contrast](../gamedev/game-design-laws-world.md#contrast) - dense and sparse, the start and the end
