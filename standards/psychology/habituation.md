# Habituation

The response to a stimulus that repeats grows weaker. It recovers after a pause, weaker and more frequent stimuli fade faster, and a different stimulus brings the response back.

## Why

A sound that repeats the same way fades into the background, which suits an ambient layer and hurts a cue the player has to notice.

## The Evidence

- Thompson and Spencer 1966, Habituation: a model phenomenon for the study of neuronal substrates of behavior, Psychological Review 73(1), 16-43, [doi:10.1037/h0022681](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0022681).
- Rankin et al. 2009, Habituation revisited, Neurobiology of Learning and Memory 92(2), 135-138, [doi:10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012). The current description.
- How solid: solid, mostly from simple reflexes.

## Limits

- A small change in pitch and length may still fall inside what habituates. Its main use is to keep a repeat from sounding mechanical, a matter of perception the research does not cover.
- Spacing a repeat lets the response recover, which the variation alone does not.

## The Laws On It

- [Soft Sound](../gamedev/game-design-laws-experience.md#soft-sound) - a repeated sound varies, a cue the player must notice is spaced, and an ambient layer may fade into the background
- [Contrast](../gamedev/game-design-laws-world.md#contrast) - busy and calm alternate, so each hits again after the other
- [No Looping Lines](../gamedev/game-design-laws-companions.md#no-looping-lines) - a line said again and again stops landing
- [Rules Broken On Purpose](../gamedev/game-design-laws-own-style.md#rules-broken-on-purpose) - a break stays rare, so it keeps landing
- The [Ma](../gamedev/game-design-patterns.md#ma) pattern - a pause lets the response to intensity recover
