# Scarcity

Things that are scarce are valued more. The effect is strongest when the scarcity comes from demand, and it works only when people notice it.

## Why

Something the player can miss gains value, if the player knows it can be missed.

## The Evidence

- Worchel, Lee and Adewole 1975, Effects of supply and demand on ratings of object value, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 32(5), 906-914, [doi:10.1037/0022-3514.32.5.906](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.32.5.906). Cookies from a jar of two were rated better than the same cookies from a jar of ten.
- Lynn 1991, Scarcity effects on value: a quantitative review of the commodity theory literature, Psychology and Marketing 8(1), 43-57, [doi:10.1002/mar.4220080105](https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.4220080105).
- Barton, Zlatevska and Oppewal 2022, Scarcity tactics in marketing: a meta-analysis of product scarcity effects on consumer purchase intentions, Journal of Retailing 98(4), 741-758, [doi:10.1016/j.jretai.2022.06.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretai.2022.06.003).
- How solid: solid for objects.

## Limits

- The research measured objects. Applying it to a line of dialog is a stretch, and no study measured whether scarcity makes people read more closely.
- A line lost for good frustrates a slow reader, and reading speed differs widely between adults (Brysbaert 2019, How many words do we read per minute? A review and meta-analysis of reading rate, Journal of Memory and Language 109, 104047, [doi:10.1016/j.jml.2019.104047](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2019.104047)). A line that waits for the player keeps the loss to a line the player chose to pass.
