# Memory Reconsolidation

Recalling a memory can open it again, and it is then stored anew, updated or stronger. It opens when what happens differs a little from what was expected. A large difference starts a new memory instead.

## Why

A player cements a mechanic by using it again in a slightly changed situation, close enough to read as the same, and different enough to surprise.

## The Evidence

- Nader, Schafe and LeDoux 2000, Fear memories require protein synthesis in the amygdala for reconsolidation after retrieval, Nature 406(6797), 722-726, [doi:10.1038/35021052](https://doi.org/10.1038/35021052). In rats, a recalled fear memory became unstable again, and blocking its storage within hours of the recall erased it.
- Sevenster, Beckers and Kindt 2013, Prediction error governs pharmacologically induced amnesia for learned fear, Science 339(6121), 830-833, [doi:10.1126/science.1231357](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1231357). In people, a memory recalled without a prediction error stayed closed, and one recalled with an error opened.
- Sevenster, Beckers and Kindt 2014, Prediction error demarcates the transition from retrieval, to reconsolidation, to new learning, Learning and Memory 21(11), 580-584, [doi:10.1101/lm.035493.114](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.035493.114). Too little error left the memory as it was, and too much started a new memory.
- Lee 2008, Memory reconsolidation mediates the strengthening of memories by additional learning, Nature Neuroscience 11(11), 1264-1266, [doi:10.1038/nn.2205](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2205). In rats, more learning strengthened a memory through its reconsolidation.
- Wymbs, Bastian and Celnik 2016, Motor skills are strengthened through reconsolidation, Current Biology 26(3), 338-343, [doi:10.1016/j.cub.2015.11.066](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2015.11.066). People who recalled a skill and then practiced a slightly altered version gained more than people who repeated it, and a version too different gained nothing.
- Elsey, Van Ast and Kindt 2018, Human memory reconsolidation: a guiding framework and critical review of the evidence, Psychological Bulletin 144(8), 797-848, [doi:10.1037/bul0000152](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000152). The current review.
- How solid: solid in animals, mixed in people.

## Limits

- Most studies use fear conditioning, many of them in animals.
- The skill result has not been replicated directly, and earlier studies of motor reconsolidation failed to replicate.
- The boundary conditions, such as how large the error may be, are vaguely defined, so a twist is sized by feel and by playtests.

## The Laws On It

- [Adopt, Repeat, Confirm (ARC)](../gamedev/game-design-laws-guidance.md#adopt-repeat-confirm-arc) - Repeat comes in a slightly changed situation, recognizably the same action
- [The Fall](../gamedev/game-design-patterns.md#the-fall) - the second climb in a slightly changed situation cements it
- [Kishōtenketsu Level](../gamedev/game-design-patterns.md#kishōtenketsu-level) - the twist in the third beat reopens what the player learned
