# Shared Experience

An experience shared with another person is felt more strongly, pleasant or not. Hardship and a goal both sides need bind people together.

## Why

What the player goes through beside a companion, a fight, a loss or a win, is what makes the bond.

## The Evidence

- Boothby, Clark and Bargh 2014, Shared experiences are amplified, Psychological Science 25(12), 2209-2216, [doi:10.1177/0956797614551162](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614551162). Chocolate tasted at the same time as another person was liked more, and bitter chocolate liked less, without a word exchanged.
- Bastian, Jetten and Ferris 2014, Pain as social glue: shared pain increases cooperation, Psychological Science 25(11), 2079-2085, [doi:10.1177/0956797614545886](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614545886). Small groups who went through pain together bonded more and cooperated more.
- Sherif 1958, Superordinate goals in the reduction of intergroup conflict, American Journal of Sociology 63(4), 349-356, [doi:10.1086/222258](https://doi.org/10.1086/222258). Rival groups came together over goals that neither could reach alone.
- How solid: solid in small lab studies. The field study of rival groups is a classic with known flaws.

## Limits

- The studies paired real people. Carrying them to a game character is an inference, resting on [Parasocial Bonds](parasocial-bonds.md).
- Shared pain was mild and brief. A game's hardship is chosen, which may weaken or change the effect.
