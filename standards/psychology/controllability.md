# Controllability

Bad outcomes a person cannot control lead to passivity, and the same outcomes under control do not. What people learn is that their actions matter, and that learning protects them later.

## Why

A risk the player can steer teaches, and a setback the player can do nothing about pushes the player away.

## The Evidence

- Maier and Seligman 2016, Learned helplessness at fifty: insights from neuroscience, Psychological Review 123(4), 349-367, [doi:10.1037/rev0000033](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000033). Passivity is the default, and control is what is learned.
- How solid: solid, mostly from animals and from strong, inescapable stress.

## Limits

- The finding is about steering. Reading a risk in advance is [Predictability](predictability.md), a separate matter.
- It says nothing against chance itself. Many players enjoy chance, and the finding concerns bad outcomes the player can do nothing about.
