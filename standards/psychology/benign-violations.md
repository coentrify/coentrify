# Benign Violations

Something is funny when it breaks how things should be and is harmless at the same time. Distance, another norm that allows it, or a weak bond with the norm broken make it harmless.

## Why

A parody lands when it breaks with its source and still reads as harmless to the player.

## The Evidence

- McGraw and Warren 2010, Benign violations: making immoral behavior funny, Psychological Science 21(8), 1141-1149, [doi:10.1177/0956797610376073](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797610376073).
- Warren and McGraw 2016, Differentiating what is humorous from what is not, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 110(3), 407-430, [doi:10.1037/pspi0000041](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000041).
- Kant and Norman 2019, You must be joking! Benign violations, power asymmetry, and humor in a broader social context, Frontiers in Psychology 10, [doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01380](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01380). A critique.
- How solid: mixed, mostly from the theory's own lab.

## Limits

- Harmless depends on the audience. A parody harmless to one player is a real offense to another who cares about its target.
