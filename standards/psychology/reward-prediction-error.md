# Reward Prediction Error

Dopamine neurons signal the gap between a reward and its expectation. A reward better than expected makes them fire, one exactly as expected stirs nothing new, and one worse than expected makes them dip.

## Why

A reward that comes as expected stops landing, so rewards grow or surprise, and a steady beat sets the expectation a surprise plays against.

## The Evidence

- Schultz, Dayan and Montague 1997, A neural substrate of prediction and reward, Science 275(5306), 1593-1599, [doi:10.1126/science.275.5306.1593](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593). Dopamine neurons in monkeys fired for an unexpected reward, moved their response to the cue that predicted it, and dipped when an expected reward failed to come.
- Schultz 2016, Dopamine reward prediction error coding, Dialogues in Clinical Neuroscience 18(1), 23-32, [doi:10.31887/DCNS.2016.18.1/wschultz](https://doi.org/10.31887/DCNS.2016.18.1/wschultz). The current review.
- How solid: solid, from animal recordings first.

## Limits

- The signal drives learning and wanting more than pleasure ([Wanting Apart From Liking](wanting-apart-from-liking.md)).
- That a game's rewards must keep growing is an inference from the signal. A reward can also land again by surprise, without growing.

## The Laws On It

- [Cadence](../gamedev/game-design-laws-progress.md#cadence) - rewards come bigger, or feel bigger, and a solo colors in the groove
