# Cognitive Load

Working memory is narrow when material is new, and what spends it on anything but understanding slows learning. Complex material is learned better in parts first, and guidance that helps a novice hinders someone who already knows.

## Why

A new player has little room to spare, so a game spends it on what matters, and gets out of the way of a player who already knows.

## The Evidence

- Sweller 1988, Cognitive load during problem solving: effects on learning, Cognitive Science 12(2), 257-285, [doi:10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4).
- Sweller, van Merriënboer and Paas 2019, Cognitive architecture and instructional design: 20 years later, Educational Psychology Review 31(2), 261-292, [doi:10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5). The current review.
- Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler and Sweller 2003, The expertise reversal effect, Educational Psychologist 38(1), 23-31, [doi:10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4).
- How solid: solid, mostly from short lessons, with the types of load hard to measure (de Jong 2010, [doi:10.1007/s11251-009-9110-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-009-9110-0)).

## Limits

- It cuts the load that adds nothing. The challenge itself is the point of a game, and the theory never asks to remove it.
- Step-by-step guidance turns into clutter for an experienced player.
