# The Goal Gradient

Effort toward a goal rises as the goal comes near. People judge the distance as the share that remains, so a goal that looks closer pulls harder.

## Why

A goal whose distance the player sees pulls the player toward it, harder as it comes near.

## The Evidence

- Hull 1932, The goal-gradient hypothesis and maze learning, Psychological Review 39(1), 25-43, [doi:10.1037/h0072640](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0072640).
- Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng 2006, The goal-gradient hypothesis resurrected, Journal of Marketing Research 43(1), 39-58, [doi:10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39). Café customers bought coffee more often as a free one came near, and a card with two stamps already given was filled faster.
- Bonezzi, Brendl and De Angelis 2011, Stuck in the middle: the psychophysics of goal pursuit, Psychological Science 22(5), 607-612, [doi:10.1177/0956797611404899](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611404899). Motivation can sag halfway.
- How solid: solid for people, from Kivetz on.

## Limits

- Early progress on a long track feels flat, and the middle can sag.
- Right after a reward, effort drops back, which is the void a next goal fills.
