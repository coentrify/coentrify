# Parasocial Bonds

People form one-sided bonds with figures they meet only through media, and respond to them as if they belonged to their own circle. Players form such bonds with game characters as well.

## Why

A companion, an ally or a rival works through the bond a player forms with it, and no law covers that bond yet.

## The Evidence

- Horton and Wohl 1956, Mass communication and para-social interaction, Psychiatry 19(3), 215-229, [doi:10.1080/00332747.1956.11023049](https://doi.org/10.1080/00332747.1956.11023049). Audiences come to feel they know a performer, in a bond that only resembles a social one.
- Lewis, Weber and Bowman 2008, They may be pixels, but they're my pixels, CyberPsychology and Behavior 11(4), 515-518, [doi:10.1089/cpb.2007.0137](https://doi.org/10.1089/cpb.2007.0137). A measure of attachment to game characters.
- Tyack and Wyeth 2017, Exploring relatedness in single-player video game play, OzCHI, 422-427, [doi:10.1145/3152771.3156149](https://doi.org/10.1145/3152771.3156149).
- Bopp, Müller, Aeschbach, Opwis and Mekler 2019, Exploring emotional attachment to game characters, CHI PLAY, 313-324, [doi:10.1145/3311350.3347169](https://doi.org/10.1145/3311350.3347169).
- How solid: solid for media figures. For game characters, young.

## Limits

- The bond is one-sided. A game character answers the player, which strains the one-sidedness, and research on it is young.

## The Laws On It

None yet. Laws for allies and companions are on the roadmap.
