# Processing Fluency

What is easy to process feels pleasant, and repeated exposure raises liking up to a point. Symmetry, a typical shape, clarity and familiarity all ease processing.

## Why

Consistency and familiar conventions make a game easy to take in, and too much sameness wears it out.

## The Evidence

- Zajonc 1968, Attitudinal effects of mere exposure, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 9(2, Pt. 2), 1-27, [doi:10.1037/h0025848](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0025848).
- Reber, Schwarz and Winkielman 2004, Processing fluency and aesthetic pleasure, Personality and Social Psychology Review 8(4), 364-382, [doi:10.1207/s15327957pspr0804_3](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327957pspr0804_3).
- Montoya, Horton, Vevea, Citkowicz and Lauber 2017, A re-examination of the mere exposure effect, Psychological Bulletin 143(5), 459-498, [doi:10.1037/bul0000085](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000085). Liking rises with exposure, then falls.
- Graf and Landwehr 2015, A dual-process perspective on fluency-based aesthetics, Personality and Social Psychology Review 19(4), 395-410, [doi:10.1177/1088868315574978](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868315574978). Fluency pleases, and interest needs a puzzle that resolves.
- How solid: solid.

## Limits

- Sameness eventually lowers liking, so one blueprint leaves room for deliberate novelty.
- That everything is polished to the same level is an inference: an outlier breaks the ease and draws attention.
