# Reciprocity

Closeness grows in turns. People like those who open up to them, open up more to those they like, and grow close as both open up step by step. Help that is received starts a bond through gratitude.

## Why

A companion who answers the player in kind, opening up as the player does, feels like a real relationship.

## The Evidence

- Collins and Miller 1994, Self-disclosure and liking: a meta-analytic review, Psychological Bulletin 116(3), 457-475, [doi:10.1037/0033-2909.116.3.457](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.3.457). People who disclose are liked more, people disclose more to those they like, and they like those they disclosed to.
- Aron, Melinat, Aron, Vallone and Bator 1997, The experimental generation of interpersonal closeness, Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 23(4), 363-377, [doi:10.1177/0146167297234003](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003). Pairs who opened up to each other step by step grew closer than pairs who made small talk.
- Algoe, Haidt and Gable 2008, Beyond reciprocity: gratitude and relationships in everyday life, Emotion 8(3), 425-429, [doi:10.1037/1528-3542.8.3.425](https://doi.org/10.1037/1528-3542.8.3.425). Gratitude for help predicted a closer relationship weeks later.
- How solid: solid for disclosure and liking. The gratitude study is one field study.

## Limits

- The closeness study built up step by step, so the pace followed both people.
- The studies paired real people. A companion's answers are written, so the effect rests on [Parasocial Bonds](parasocial-bonds.md).
