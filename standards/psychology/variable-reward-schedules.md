# Variable Reward Schedules

A reward that comes after an unpredictable number of actions keeps behavior high and steady, and slow to fade. The same schedule is what makes paid random rewards compulsive.

## Why

Irregular rewards keep a player exploring, and the line between curiosity and compulsion runs through this finding.

## The Evidence

- Ferster and Skinner 1957, Schedules of Reinforcement, Appleton-Century-Crofts. The schedules, in animals.
- Zendle and Cairns 2018, Video game loot boxes are linked to problem gambling, PLOS ONE 13(11), e0206767, [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0206767](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0206767).
- Garea, Drummond, Sauer, Hall and Williams 2021, Meta-analysis of the relationship between problem gambling, excessive gaming and loot box spending, International Gambling Studies 21(3), 460-479, [doi:10.1080/14459795.2021.1914705](https://doi.org/10.1080/14459795.2021.1914705). Spending on loot boxes goes with problem gambling, the cause unresolved.
- How solid: solid in animals. In games, the evidence links paid random rewards to problems, without showing cause.

## Limits

- The evidence on harm covers paid, random rewards. It says nothing against a find that rewards curiosity.
- What separates the two is not settled research. Real money, a reward detached from the act, and play that runs on after the fun has gone are the warning signs.
