# Effort And The IKEA Effect

People value what they made themselves more than the same thing made for them, as long as the effort succeeded. Effort that fails or is wiped out adds no value. People also judge a thing by the effort they see went into it.

## Why

Earned progress is worth more to the player than progress handed over, and only when the effort lands.

## The Evidence

- Norton, Mochon and Ariely 2012, The IKEA effect: when labor leads to love, Journal of Consumer Psychology 22(3), 453-460, [doi:10.1016/j.jcps.2011.08.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2011.08.002). People valued boxes, origami and Lego they built themselves close to an expert's work. The effect vanished when the work was destroyed or left unfinished.
- Aronson and Mills 1959, The effect of severity of initiation on liking for a group, The Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology 59(2), 177-181, [doi:10.1037/h0047195](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0047195). The older account, where suffering raised liking. It rests on one study, and real groups have not shown it well.
- Kruger, Wirtz, Van Boven and Altermatt 2004, The effort heuristic, Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 40(1), 91-98, [doi:10.1016/S0022-1031(03)00065-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-1031(03)00065-9). Poems, paintings and armor were rated better when more time and effort seemed to have gone into them.
- How solid: the IKEA effect is solid. Effort justification is weaker. The effort heuristic rests on a few studies.

## Limits

- Neither finding supports grind or punishment as a source of value. The value comes from effort that succeeds.
- The IKEA effect measured objects. Carrying it to progress in a game is an inference.
- The effort heuristic is about the effort others see in a maker's work, a separate route from a person's own effort.
