# Wanting Apart From Liking

Wanting a thing and liking it run on separate systems in the brain. Wanting is the pull toward a reward, liking is the pleasure it gives, and the two can come apart.

## Why

Desire can be built as something of its own, apart from the pleasure a reward gives, and the same mechanism, pushed too far, is what makes a habit compulsive.

## The Evidence

- Berridge and Robinson 1998, What is the role of dopamine in reward: hedonic impact, reward learning, or incentive salience?, Brain Research Reviews 28(3), 309-369, [doi:10.1016/S0165-0173(98)00019-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0165-0173(98)00019-8).
- Berridge and Robinson 2016, Liking, wanting, and the incentive-sensitization theory of addiction, American Psychologist 71(8), 670-679, [doi:10.1037/amp0000059](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000059).
- Pool, Sennwald, Delplanque, Brosch and Sander 2016, Measuring wanting and liking from animals to humans, Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews 63, 124-142, [doi:10.1016/j.neubiorev.2016.01.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2016.01.006). In people, the two are harder to measure apart.
- How solid: solid in animals, mixed in people.

## Limits

- It says nothing about order. A reward that was never wanted can still be liked.
- Wanting driven by cues can outgrow liking, which is the core of addiction. A law that builds desire holds it to what the player still enjoys.
