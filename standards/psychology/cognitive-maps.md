# Cognitive Maps

People and animals learn an inner picture of a place that lets them find new routes. In people the picture is patchy, closer to a network of places than to a map, and it differs widely between people.

## Why

A world is found through the picture the player builds of it, and that picture rests on a few strong, clear elements.

## The Evidence

- Tolman 1948, Cognitive maps in rats and men, Psychological Review 55(4), 189-208, [doi:10.1037/h0061626](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0061626).
- Lynch 1960, The Image of the City, MIT Press. Paths, edges, districts, nodes and landmarks, from interviews and sketch maps.
- Warren, Rothman, Schnapp and Ericson 2017, Wormholes in virtual space: from cognitive maps to cognitive graphs, Cognition 166, 152-163, [doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2017.05.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2017.05.020).
- How solid: solid that the picture exists. Lynch's elements are a well-used taxonomy from a small study.

## Limits

- Players differ widely, and some always need a map or a compass.
- A maze or a layout that breaks distance distorts what players remember.
