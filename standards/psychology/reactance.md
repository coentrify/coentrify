# Reactance

When people feel a freedom threatened, by an order, a push or a message pressed on them, they push back. They grow angry, discount the message, and lean toward what was forbidden.

## Why

A game that orders, pushes or preaches meets resistance, and a player who chooses meets none.

## The Evidence

- Brehm 1966, A Theory of Psychological Reactance, Academic Press.
- Rains 2013, The nature of psychological reactance revisited: a meta-analytic review, Human Communication Research 39(1), 47-73, [doi:10.1111/j.1468-2958.2012.01443.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2012.01443.x). Reactance is anger mixed with negative thoughts, and it lowers persuasion.
- Steindl, Jonas, Sittenthaler, Traut-Mattausch and Greenberg 2015, Understanding psychological reactance, Zeitschrift für Psychologie 223(4), 205-214, [doi:10.1027/2151-2604/a000222](https://doi.org/10.1027/2151-2604/a000222). The current review.
- How solid: solid, mostly from messages about health and behavior.

## Limits

- Most studies test persuasive messages, such as health campaigns. Carrying them to a quest or a level is an inference.
- A limit that comes with a meaningful reason meets less resistance ([Autonomy, Competence And Relatedness](autonomy-competence-and-relatedness.md)).

## The Laws On It

- [Embedded Relevance](../gamedev/game-design-laws-story.md#embedded-relevance) - a message is experienced, never pressed on the player
- [Tracks, Never Instructs](../gamedev/game-design-laws-guidance.md#tracks-never-instructs) - a quest log tracks, and never orders
- [Compulsion Is Considered](../gamedev/game-design-laws-values.md#compulsion-is-considered) - being made to do aggravating work meets resistance
