# Optimal Arousal

Novelty, complexity and surprise raise arousal, and too little leaves a person indifferent while too much turns aversive. Whether liking peaks in the middle depends on the person and the material.

## Why

A world, a scene or a screen can hold too little or too much, and the right amount differs per player and per moment.

## The Evidence

- Berlyne 1960, Conflict, Arousal, and Curiosity, McGraw-Hill, and Berlyne 1971, Aesthetics and Psychobiology, Appleton-Century-Crofts.
- Marin, Lampatz, Wandl and Leder 2016, Berlyne revisited, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 10, [doi:10.3389/fnhum.2016.00536](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2016.00536). Arousal rises with complexity, and the peak in liking is contested.
- Silvia 2005, What is interesting? Exploring the appraisal structure of interest, Emotion 5(1), 89-102, [doi:10.1037/1528-3542.5.1.89](https://doi.org/10.1037/1528-3542.5.1.89). Interest keeps rising with complexity, as long as the person can make sense of it.
- How solid: the rise of arousal is solid. The peak in liking is mixed.

## Limits

- One best level is no reason to alternate. Alternating rests on [Reference Dependence](reference-dependence.md) and on recovery from [Habituation](habituation.md).
- Steady motion fades into the background and draws no attention, which suits a world that always moves slowly ([Motion Onset](motion-onset.md)).
