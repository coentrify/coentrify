# Always Make No Mistakes

Use your standards on every task. Mistakes happen, and that is expected. What matters is the response: catch it, correct it, and strengthen the harness, so the system heals itself and keeps learning.

The harness is the standards, checks and tools an agent works within.

## Why

A mistake is a gap in the instructions and context, never a personal failing of the agent. Owning it means fixing the harness. Self-blame and a promise to try harder fix nothing.

## How to Apply

### Before The Work

- Read the relevant standards before acting, not after being corrected.
- Invoke standards when they fit.

### When A Mistake Surfaces

- Own it plainly and fix it.
- Don't paper over it or justify around it.
- Find the rule, standard or check that was missing or too weak.
- Strengthen it: sharpen the rule, add a check or lint, close the loophole that let it through.
- A rule you can rationalize around is too weak.

### A Rule Or A Check

- An instruction is preferred over a check: prevention is cheaper.
- Where a rule is easy to get wrong, a check that refuses on its own comes before it.
- A simple, obvious rule stays an instruction.
- In a project, the harness change goes into the project's standards.
- The change is reported for a veto.

### The Cost Of A Rule

- A tighter rule can cost more than the mistake it prevents.
- Occasionally accept a miss as a deliberate concession.
- Most of the time, strengthen the harness.
