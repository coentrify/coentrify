# Game Flavors: World Structure

How the world is laid out, and how it opens up to the player. These are the seven main structures, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Structures Mix

- Structures mix by scope: an open world with linear dungeons, or handcrafted places among generated islands: `- World Structure: Generated Each Run - the islands`, `- World Structure: Hub And Spokes - the landmarks`.
- The laws hold over every structure: a gate has a reason in the world ([No Artificial Gates](game-design-laws-world.md#no-artificial-gates)), the player finds the way ([Wayfinding](wayfinding.md)), looking around finds something ([Exploration Is Rewarded](game-design-laws-experience.md#exploration-is-rewarded)), and a hub can double as the safe haven ([Contrast](game-design-laws-world.md#contrast)).

## Linear

One path from start to end, place after place.

Example: a train journey across a continent, one carriage after the next.

Weighs: tight pacing and authored moments, and little freedom.

## Hub And Spokes

A central place the player keeps returning to, with areas branching off it.

Example: a tavern at a crossroads, with roads to five valleys.

Weighs: a home to come back to and a clear structure, and the hub can turn into a menu.

## Interconnected

One world whose parts loop back on each other, opened up by new abilities.

Example: the locked door in the first cave opens once the player can breathe underwater.

Weighs: discovery and shortcuts that reward memory ([Cognitive Maps](../psychology/cognitive-maps.md)), and backtracking can tire.

## Open

The whole world is reachable early, and explored in any order.

Example: from the first hill the player can see the volcano, and walk there.

Weighs: freedom and exploring, and it can feel empty and directionless, so something to do and what is coming stay in view ([Always Something To Do](game-design-laws-experience.md#always-something-to-do), [Know What Is Coming](game-design-laws-progress.md#know-what-is-coming)).

## Arenas

A set of handcrafted stages, one chosen or drawn for each match.

Example: a rooftop, a ferry deck and a frozen pond, one drawn for each match.

Weighs: matches stay fresh and fair, mastered stage by stage, and there is no world to live in.

## Generated Once

A world generated from a seed, and kept as the player shapes it.

Example: a seed grows an island chain of its own, and it stays as the player builds on it.

Weighs: a world that is the player's own and lasts, and fewer authored landmarks and meaning.

## Generated Each Run

A world generated fresh each time, like a new level.

Example: each dive finds a new reef.

Weighs: endless variety and replay, and the places blur, with little authored meaning.
