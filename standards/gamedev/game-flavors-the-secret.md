# Game Flavors: The Secret

What the world's secret turns out to be, in a game built on a mystery. [Fair Surprise](game-design-laws-world.md#fair-surprise) sets how the twist lands, and the flavor sets what it is. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## Every Theory Is True

Every wild explanation the world hints at is true, and the ones who sound crazy are right.

Example: the old man who swears the lighthouse talks to ships is right.

Conflicts: The Mundane Truth, One True Theory and Never Confirmed.

## The Mundane Truth

The uncanny has an ordinary cause: people.

Example: the haunting is the landlord, driving the tenants out.

Conflicts: Every Theory Is True, One True Theory and Never Confirmed.

## One True Theory

Most explanations are wrong, and the player sorts out the one that is right.

Example: three townsfolk each blame a different curse, and one of them is right.

Conflicts: Every Theory Is True, The Mundane Truth and Never Confirmed.

## Never Confirmed

The ambiguity holds to the end.

Example: the end leaves open whether the visitor was ever real.

Conflicts: Every Theory Is True, The Mundane Truth and One True Theory.
