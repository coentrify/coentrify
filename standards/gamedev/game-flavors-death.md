# Game Flavors: Death

What happens when a character dies, and what each choice weighs. The flavors mix: a game picks one per kind of character, such as the player, the story's characters and the nameless ones. [A Loss Is Grieved](game-design-laws-companions.md#a-loss-is-grieved) weighs a companion's loss, and the flavor sets whether a loss can happen. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## Permanent Death

A death sticks, and carries its weight.

Example: a fallen party member stays gone, and so does their seat at the campfire.

Weighs: killing becomes a real choice with lasting consequences, and the story has to hold up without anyone the player can kill.

## Nobody Really Dies

The hurt always come back, and the player may learn the rule as a surprise.

Example: in a world of toys, a broken toy is always mended by morning.

Weighs: the world stays whole, and a fall loses its stakes.

## Respawn

Coming back is a convention of the game. Whether anyone explains it is up to [Conventions](game-flavors-conventions.md).

Example: the player reappears at the last campfire after a fall.

Weighs: play keeps flowing, and a death means little.

## Scripted Deaths Only

Death happens in the story, never in play.

Example: the mentor dies in a cutscene, and no one in play ever can.

Weighs: every death lands as written, and the player has no say over one.

## Unkillable

The characters who matter cannot die. A blow leaves them down for a moment, and the story goes on.

Example: the blacksmith shrugs off a stray arrow and goes back to the forge.

Weighs: the story goes on as planned, and its plot armor shows.
