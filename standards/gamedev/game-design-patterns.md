# Game Design Patterns

Named patterns a game can use, each with what it does and when it fits: devices proven to work, never all there are. A game uses a pattern where it fits. A law holds everywhere, unless there is a very good reason ([Game Design Laws](game-design-laws.md)).

## Why

A named pattern is reused on purpose and recognized when it comes up, so it never has to be invented again.

## No-Gear Level

A No-Gear level takes the player's gear away for a while: the tools, the money, the places, the network. The player plays the core loop again from nothing, in a limited place.

- It is an intermezzo, a disruption in the middle of the game, never the end game.
- The loop the player knows carries it, so nothing new has to be taught: the player proves the skill without the gear.
- The loss answers something the player did, such as getting caught, never a twist out of nothing ([Fair Surprise](game-design-laws-world.md#fair-surprise)).
- It ends with the gear coming back, which makes what was built feel earned a second time ([Earned Progress](game-design-laws-progress.md#earned-progress)).
- A game may open with one as well: an opening that shows a later setup, then takes it away.

Rests on: [Effort And The IKEA Effect](../psychology/effort-and-the-ikea-effect.md).

## The Fall

Well into the game, the player loses what they built, and builds it again by playing. It is the No-Gear level's harder sibling: the gear does not come back, and the rebuild is the point.

- The loss answers something the player did, or a turn the story set up ([Fair Surprise](game-design-laws-world.md#fair-surprise)). It comes in the middle, never at the very start or the end.
- The rebuild is quicker and cheaper than the first climb: what the player knows carries over, and shortcuts open, so it never feels like a do-over punishment.
- Something small survives, a keepsake or one ally, and what the game needs comes back ([No Softlock](game-design-laws-progress.md#no-softlock)).
- The second climb runs in a slightly changed situation, recognizably the same, and can turn out better than the first.
- Chosen, it is prestige or new game plus: the player gives up what they built for a lasting edge ([Start And End](game-design-laws-progress.md#start-and-end)).
- It is the central crisis of Rags To Riches ([Game Flavors: Plot](game-flavors-plot.md)), and it often takes an empire or allies ([Game Flavors: Power](game-flavors-power.md)).

Rests on: [Effort And The IKEA Effect](../psychology/effort-and-the-ikea-effect.md), [Loss Aversion](../psychology/loss-aversion.md), [Memory Reconsolidation](../psychology/memory-reconsolidation.md).

## Kishōtenketsu Level

A level built in four beats around one idea, after the old four-part form: introduce it, develop it, twist it, conclude it. It is [Adopt, Repeat, Confirm](game-design-laws-guidance.md#adopt-repeat-confirm-arc) inside one level.

- Ki introduces the idea in a safe spot, where nothing can go wrong.
- Shō develops it, a little harder.
- Ten twists it, used in a way the player did not see coming, and still answering the idea ([Fair Surprise](game-design-laws-world.md#fair-surprise)).
- Ketsu concludes it with a last flourish, and the idea is retired. The next level brings a new one.
- One new idea per level, so each one lands. The story's own four-part form is in [Game Flavors: Story Structure](game-flavors-story-structure.md).

Rests on: [Cognitive Load](../psychology/cognitive-load.md), [Memory Reconsolidation](../psychology/memory-reconsolidation.md), [Retrieval Practice](../psychology/retrieval-practice.md).

## Ma

Ma (間), from Japanese art, is a designed pause: an interval between moments of intensity that gives them their shape. The player does little, and the moment still holds what came before and what is coming. Ma is an aesthetic concept more than a finding or a law, so a game uses it as a choice of style.

- The pause is placed on purpose and charged, never a moment of what now ([Always Something To Do](game-design-laws-experience.md#always-something-to-do)).
- The world keeps breathing through it, so it never reads as a frozen screen ([Alive World, Static Actions](game-design-laws-ui.md#alive-world-static-actions)).
- It is the rest in the groove ([Cadence](game-design-laws-progress.md#cadence)), and it gives the sparse its meaning ([Contrast](game-design-laws-world.md#contrast)).
- Where the player can step away, the pause doubles as a natural break ([Natural Breaks](game-design-laws-experience.md#natural-breaks)).

Rests on, as the nearest findings: [Hedonic Adaptation](../psychology/hedonic-adaptation.md), [Habituation](../psychology/habituation.md), [Attention Restoration](../psychology/attention-restoration.md).
