# Game Flavors: Story Delivery

How the story reaches the player. The story's shape is another question, and this layer is only how it is told. These are the six main channels, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Channels Mix

- Channels mix: a game picks a main channel, and accents where they fit, each naming its scope after a dash: `- Story Delivery: Environmental Storytelling - main`, `- Story Delivery: Found Documents - the archive`.
- The story laws hold over every channel ([Show, Don't Tell](game-design-laws-story.md#show-dont-tell), [Worth Reading](game-design-laws-story.md#worth-reading), [Dialog Gives Context](game-design-laws-story.md#dialog-gives-context), [Once Per Save](game-design-laws-experience.md#once-per-save)).
- A channel never comes in over a law. Companions who voice parts of the hero's ego stay out ([Characters Of Their Own](game-design-laws-companions.md#characters-of-their-own)).

## Cutscenes

The story plays out in scenes the player watches.

Example: after the bridge falls, a scene shows the rival watching from the cliff.

Weighs: cinematic control over the moment, and control taken from the player, so a scene plays once per save and [Full Control](game-design-laws-experience.md#full-control) limits how far it goes.

## Environmental Storytelling

The world tells the story, through places, objects and traces.

Example: a kitchen with two cups, one smashed, and a packed suitcase by the door.

Weighs: the story is discovered rather than told, and it is easy to miss.

## Found Documents

Letters, logs and notes the player finds.

Example: a ship's log, found page by page, tells why the crew left.

Weighs: depth at the player's own pace, and reading interrupts play and can feel like homework.

## Dialogue

Characters tell the story in conversation, sometimes with choices.

Example: the innkeeper's small talk changes once the player has met her brother.

Weighs: character and voice, and the words pile up.

## Narrator

A voice tells the story as it is played, reacting to the player.

Example: a gruff voice remarks on each step the player takes, as it happens.

Weighs: personality and a live link to the player's actions, and one voice can grate over a long game.

## Wordless

No words at all: picture, sound and action carry the story.

Example: a chase told only in lantern light and music.

Weighs: universal, with nothing to translate, and it limits how complex the story gets.
