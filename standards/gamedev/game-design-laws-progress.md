# Game Design Laws: Progress

Laws about the shape of the whole game: its length, its completion and the progress the player makes. The index and the way a game uses them are in [Game Design Laws](game-design-laws.md).

## Start And End

The game has a start and an end. That also counts for prestige and new game plus.

Rests on: [The Peak-End Rule](../psychology/the-peak-end-rule.md).

## As Long As It Is Interesting

The game is as long as it is interesting, not the other way around.

- No level, scene or story beat has a target length. Everything is there as it should be
- Nothing is stretched to fill a playtime, and nothing is cut to meet one
- A fast player goes fast
- The game ends on a high, while the player still wants more. The player walks away with a strong sense of closure and achievement, and a way back in: the itch to do another loop, even when nothing is left to do, because the player misses it
- The end is a soft closure, never a hard what now: play may go on after it, and slowly matters less. What comes next fits the climax: a minor task right after it leaves a gap
- A level or a loop ends on a high too, and makes a natural break ([Natural Breaks](game-design-laws-experience.md#natural-breaks))

Rests on: [Hedonic Adaptation](../psychology/hedonic-adaptation.md), [The Peak-End Rule](../psychology/the-peak-end-rule.md).

## Doable 100%

Completing the game 100% is reasonably doable. No orthogonal gameplay, like a parkour section in an automation game, that demands a different skill for completion. Pride in 100% comes from playing the entire game and doing all the side quests. A player grinding, bored, because of one missing side quest means the side challenges were designed wrong.

Rests on: [Autonomy, Competence And Relatedness](../psychology/autonomy-competence-and-relatedness.md).

## No Try Hard

Relevant for: a game made for every type of player, never a competitive one.

Beating the game normally never requires playing try hard. The game supports every type of player, the completionist and the casual alike, by staying flexible and never forcing a play style. Optimizing is there for the player who wants it.

Rests on: [Autonomy, Competence And Relatedness](../psychology/autonomy-competence-and-relatedness.md).

## Bands, Not Bullseyes

Relevant for: skill checks, timing and crafting.

Success is a band, never a bullseye. A hit lands in a hitbox, never on a point, and a short grace period absorbs lag and thinking time. A beginner scrapes through, an expert optimizes, and any method that lands in the band counts ([No Try Hard](#no-try-hard)).

Rests on: [Controllability](../psychology/controllability.md), [Autonomy, Competence And Relatedness](../psychology/autonomy-competence-and-relatedness.md), [Fitts's Law](../psychology/fitts-law.md).

## Desire Before Reward

The player wants a thing before they can have it. A wreck on the horizon is seen long before it can be reached ([Always Something To Do](game-design-laws-experience.md#always-something-to-do)). The wanting makes the way there worth playing, a dull task included. A reward that comes before the wanting is enjoyed, and pulls the player nowhere. It also spends the wanting: what the player already has can no longer be earned, and the gap between the start and the end shrinks ([Earned Progress](#earned-progress)).

Rests on: [Wanting Apart From Liking](../psychology/wanting-apart-from-liking.md), [Reference Dependence](../psychology/reference-dependence.md).

## Earned Progress

The player feels the progress made, so the gap between where the player starts and ends is as big as it can be: the player starts with as little as possible and earns the rest. Nothing that can be earned is handed over. What comes for free is worth nothing. A free first step that starts a climb is no gift but the climb's first rung.

- Quality of life is earned too. A task is done by hand first and automated later, so losing friction is progress.
- Upgrades come in small, regular steps that are felt in play.
- The player still remembers the humble beginnings, and the game brings back a reminder where and when it chooses: a flashback, a level without gear, a reference to the start, or an ending cutscene that saves it all up. Letting several things come back in cycles is a good pattern.
- Relevant for an economy: every step up brings a new risk beside the new unlock.

Rests on: [Effort And The IKEA Effect](../psychology/effort-and-the-ikea-effect.md), [Hedonic Adaptation](../psychology/hedonic-adaptation.md), [Reference Dependence](../psychology/reference-dependence.md).

## Lasting Impact

What the player does and decides has a lasting effect on the game's progress.

Rests on: [Autonomy, Competence And Relatedness](../psychology/autonomy-competence-and-relatedness.md).

## Emergent Gameplay

Systems whose mere existence opens possibilities for the player, play styles among them, rather than features that each do one thing.

Rests on: [Autonomy, Competence And Relatedness](../psychology/autonomy-competence-and-relatedness.md), [Effort And The IKEA Effect](../psychology/effort-and-the-ikea-effect.md).

## Know What Is Coming

The player always knows what is coming in the short future: the next upgrade, the next unlock, the distance to the next goal, and when it is the end. There is always something coming, so the next upgrade or level is always looming. Knowing needs no overview or progress bar: the world can show it, by a billboard, a glimpse or a line of dialog. Nothing unlocks before it is introduced ([Adopt, Repeat, Confirm](game-design-laws-guidance.md#adopt-repeat-confirm-arc)). The world still keeps its secrets ([Fair Surprise](game-design-laws-world.md#fair-surprise)).

Rests on: [The Goal Gradient](../psychology/the-goal-gradient.md).

## Cadence

Good art has an implicit cadence, and without it there is no harmony ([In Harmony](game-design-laws-own-style.md#in-harmony)). Play runs on a groove: a beat of loops and rewards the player comes to expect, nested in larger ones, bigger and bigger. The first loop is small and teaches the mechanics in miniature ([Adopt, Repeat, Confirm (ARC)](game-design-laws-guidance.md#adopt-repeat-confirm-arc)), and the loop expands as the game goes on. As the mechanics grow familiar, time moves faster and the player has more patience for the next reward, and each reward comes bigger, or feels bigger, to land the same. A steady groove leaves room for a solo that colors it in.

Rests on: [Reward Prediction Error](../psychology/reward-prediction-error.md), [Hedonic Adaptation](../psychology/hedonic-adaptation.md), [Reference Dependence](../psychology/reference-dependence.md), [Cognitive Load](../psychology/cognitive-load.md), [Predictability](../psychology/predictability.md).

## No Softlock

Whatever the game's flavors, a mistake never makes the game unwinnable. An item the game needs that leaves the world, falls out of reach or is used up comes back, and a scarce item is never the only way through. The player can fail, and can always go on.

Rests on: [Controllability](../psychology/controllability.md), [Loss Aversion](../psychology/loss-aversion.md).
