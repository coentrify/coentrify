# Game Flavors: Plot

What the story is about at heart: the kind of change it tells. [Story Structure](game-flavors-story-structure.md) is the shape, and the plot fills it. These are Christopher Booker's seven basic plots and the two he adds, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Plots Mix

- Plots mix by scope: a main plot and its subplots: `- Plot: Rags To Riches - main`, `- Plot: Rebellion Against The One - the town`.
- Mystery chains with the layers below it: the plot is a question, [The Secret](game-flavors-the-secret.md) is the answer, and [Reveal](game-flavors-reveal.md) is how it comes out.

## Overcoming The Monster

A threat is faced and defeated.

Example: a fishing village rises against the sea serpent that takes a boat every winter.

Weighs: a clear goal and a release, and a villain that risks going flat.

## Rags To Riches

From nothing to everything, with a crisis in between where it is all lost.

Example: a street sweeper builds a cleaning empire, loses it in a fire, and builds it again.

Weighs: growth the player feels, which suits progression, and it needs a real fall to matter.

## The Quest

A journey toward a goal, with companions and trials.

Example: three friends cross the mountains to find the last seed of a vanished tree.

Weighs: purpose and travel, and it can turn into a checklist.

## Voyage And Return

Into a strange world and back, changed.

Example: a girl falls through the ice into a kingdom of frozen fish, and comes home wiser.

Weighs: wonder and a changed homecoming, and the strange world has to be rich.

## Comedy

Confusion and misunderstanding resolved into harmony. A plot, and no tone: [Tone](game-flavors-tone.md) holds Comic.

Example: two rival bakers swap recipe books by mistake, and the town's wedding cake goes wrong until it goes right.

Weighs: warmth and resolution, and it can feel contrived.

## Tragedy

A flaw leads to a fall.

Example: a gifted clockmaker who must make everything perfect drives away everyone who loves him.

Weighs: depth and weight, and a fall the player cannot stop can feel unfair, so it needs closure.

## Rebirth

Someone trapped in darkness is redeemed.

Example: a bitter innkeeper is thawed by the orphan who turns up on his doorstep.

Weighs: a strong emotional payoff, and a slow one.

## Rebellion Against The One

Standing up to an all-powerful order.

Example: a clerk in an all-seeing ministry quietly starts misfiling the files.

Weighs: the player as the resistance, which empowers, and the order has to feel overwhelming.

## Mystery

A question that has to be solved.

Example: a missing violinist, and a town where everyone heard her last song differently.

Weighs: the pull of curiosity ([Curiosity As An Information Gap](../psychology/curiosity-as-an-information-gap.md)), and the answer has to satisfy ([Fair Surprise](game-design-laws-world.md#fair-surprise)).
