# Game Design Laws: Experience

Laws about what the player lives through from moment to moment. The index and the way a game uses them are in [Game Design Laws](game-design-laws.md).

## Always Something To Do

The player always has something to do. Short term, the pull to expand and explore. Mid term, the next objective and the one after it: the goals form a queue, so reaching one never leaves the player in the void that follows the last episode of a series. The queue brings no pressure, only something to want next. Long term, a dot on the horizon. When closing in on it, the outline of the next dot is already visible. The ultimate goal is not revealed too soon, but is visible from early mid-game. That holds at every skill level: a skilled player who goes fast never runs out of things to do. Things in the world take time, a craft, a train on its way, the weather or the time of day, and meanwhile there is still something to do, so play never falls into a moment of what now. A technical wait, such as loading, is as short as it can be, and shows that it is working. Nothing new to pursue for a moment is not nothing to do: there is the state to check and the next choice to think about, such as the balance when there is nothing to buy yet. Leave space for thinking, just not too much when the player already knows what to do. The better the player plays, the less time there is to think.

Rests on: [Flow](../psychology/flow.md), [Resuming An Interrupted Task](../psychology/resuming-an-interrupted-task.md), [The Goal Gradient](../psychology/the-goal-gradient.md), [Response Time Limits](../psychology/response-time-limits.md).

## Exploration Is Rewarded

A player who looks around, opens something or goes somewhere unasked always finds something there: a thing to build, a clue, a secret. Curiosity pays, so the explorer keeps exploring.

Rests on: [Curiosity As An Information Gap](../psychology/curiosity-as-an-information-gap.md), [Variable Reward Schedules](../psychology/variable-reward-schedules.md).

## Full Control

The player feels in full control. Nothing is decided and nothing happens without the player taking action. A surprise of the world answers the player ([Fair Surprise](game-design-laws-world.md#fair-surprise)).

Rests on: [Autonomy, Competence And Relatedness](../psychology/autonomy-competence-and-relatedness.md).

## Respect

The player feels respected. No dumb tutorials. Feeling the need for a tutorial is a sign that something is not obvious.

Rests on: [Autonomy, Competence And Relatedness](../psychology/autonomy-competence-and-relatedness.md), [Rewards And Feedback](../psychology/rewards-and-feedback.md).

## Speedrun Ready

A returning player speedrunning the game has just as good an experience as a player starting for the first time.

## Once Per Save

A story cutscene plays once per save, the first time its moment comes. Every later run of that save goes past it without stopping. A replayed run or a prestige never replays it, and a new save plays it again. A progression minigame plays once per save too: a later run passes a won minigame at once, its outcome given.

## AFK Safe

The player can always be away, planned, unplanned or urgent, without negative effects from the game state. This goes two ways: nothing dies, nothing is lost and no events are missed while away, and nothing progresses beyond what the player set running. No blocking pop-ups that stop the game until OK is clicked.

- What the player set running keeps running until the player pauses it: a factory produces, perhaps until its materials run out, and currency grows.
- Nothing upgrades on its own, and no raid or heavy debuff lands while the player is away.

Rests on: [Loss Aversion](../psychology/loss-aversion.md).

## Easy Pause

Pausing is always easy.

Rests on: [Hedonic Adaptation](../psychology/hedonic-adaptation.md).

## Soft Sound

The mix is soft. A repeated sound varies a bit in pitch and length. A layer belongs to its scene: it starts with the scene and stops when the scene leaves. Playtest feedback on sound asks for softer, lower and more varied, never for louder.

Rests on: [Habituation](../psychology/habituation.md).

## Fun First

Player experience is priority number one. The game is fun, and so is the main gameplay loop.

- A test of the design: a good game clips well. Plain footage of the game being played holds a viewer.
- Footage that does not hold up points at a change in the design, never at more steering.
- Nothing is designed for the clip. A good game produces those moments by itself.

Rests on: [Flow](../psychology/flow.md).

## Loop First

The core loop is fun in a greybox, before anything is built on it. Looks, content and story do not rescue a loop that is no fun bare. The law is also the gate of the Prototype phase ([Game Phases: Concept](game-phases-concept.md#prototype)).

Rests on: [Autonomy, Competence And Relatedness](../psychology/autonomy-competence-and-relatedness.md).
