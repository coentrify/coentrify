# Game Design Laws: Own Style

Laws about what makes the game its own: its assets and its inventions. The index and the way a game uses them are in [Game Design Laws](game-design-laws.md).

## No Asset Flips

Every asset is the game's own, and nothing looks generic. The style gives the game its identity.

## Blueprint First

Things of one kind come from one blueprint: its style, its guidelines and its artistic choices, cartoony or realistic say. Ten buildings from one blueprint cohere.

## Shown Together

Everything of one kind is shown in one place: a styleguide, a dev level, a page with every icon. Together, a style can be judged.

## Generate, Never Reuse

Making an asset is a commodity now. Generate your own, and never reuse a pack. Music is the exception: a track may come from a library, licensed for the game, unless the music is where the game is unique ([Unique Where It Counts](#unique-where-it-counts)).

## Unique Where It Counts

The game is unique in what makes it that game, and the rest follows best practice ([Unique Where It Counts](../architecture/unique-where-it-counts.md)). A narrative game never reinvents the inventory, unless the inventory is a core element.

## References Stay References

Relevant for: references and parody.

A reference stays a reference: a parody, a spin or a nod, never a copy that infringes or invites legal trouble.

- A famous real person gets a parody name only.
- A character from a film or a series keeps a name that is generic, such as John Johnson. A distinctive one, such as Harvey Specter, gets a spin, and so does a name in the gray area between.
- Likeness means the character, never the actor: the character is recreated in the game's own style, and resembles the original through traits such as a beard or an outfit, never the actor's face.
- References are extra, never what the game stands on: most of it stays funny for a player who misses the reference. Heavy parody is the exception, since it needs the reference.
