# Game Flavors: Art Style

The look of the game at its base. The game's style starts from this pick, and its fields refine it: realism, detail, palette, materials and light ([Asset Layers](asset-layers.md)). These are the nine main styles, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Styles Mix

- Styles mix by scope: hand-drawn characters on 3D backgrounds, say: `- Art Style: Hand Drawn - the characters`, `- Art Style: Painterly - the world`.
- The laws hold over every style: the senses carry one message ([In Harmony](game-design-laws-own-style.md#in-harmony)), things of one kind come from one blueprint ([Blueprint First](game-design-laws-own-style.md#blueprint-first)), every asset is the game's own ([No Asset Flips](game-design-laws-own-style.md#no-asset-flips), [Generate, Never Reuse](game-design-laws-own-style.md#generate-never-reuse)), and the look is unique where it counts ([Unique Where It Counts](game-design-laws-own-style.md#unique-where-it-counts)).

## Pixel Art

Low-resolution sprites, every pixel placed.

Example: a 16-color harbor where each wave is three white pixels.

Weighs: charm and clarity at a lower cost per asset, and it reads as retro, and frame-by-frame animation adds up.

## Low Poly

Few polygons, with visible facets and flat shading.

Example: faceted hills and cone-shaped trees under a single sun.

Weighs: fast work and clear silhouettes, and it can look generic.

## Simple Stylized

Few polygons with smooth shading, flat colors without detailed textures, and caricatured proportions.

Example: a town of rounded, noseless people with oversized heads, under plain daylight.

Weighs: cheap, readable and charming, and it runs anywhere, and it reads as cheap at first glance, until the style grows on the player.

## Hand Drawn

Illustrated, painted or inked by hand.

Example: every frame of the fox inked and washed in watercolor.

Weighs: unique and warm, and costly for every frame.

## Cel Shaded

3D with flat bands of shadow and dark outlines, the look of animation painted on celluloid.

Example: a 3D knight with black outlines and two tones of shadow.

Weighs: timeless, it ages well, and flat light limits the mood.

## Painterly

Brushstroke textures, as if a painting moved.

Example: a meadow whose grass moves in visible brushstrokes.

Weighs: beautiful and distinct, and hard to keep consistent across assets.

## Realistic

Photographic detail and light.

Example: rain that beads on a car's paint and mirrors the neon.

Weighs: immersion and spectacle, and the most costly, the fastest to age, and at risk of the uncanny valley.

## Voxel

Built from small cubes.

Example: a castle of cubes where the player can knock out every one.

Weighs: a world that can be built and broken, and blocky by nature.

## Crafted Materials

The world looks made of paper, felt, clay or yarn.

Example: a town of felt and cardboard, with visible stitches.

Weighs: tactile charm, and costly to keep consistent.
