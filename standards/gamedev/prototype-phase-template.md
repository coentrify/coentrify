# Prototype Phase Template

The Prototype phase page holds what the prototype tries and what it should answer, in fixed sections beside its goal, its gate and its Done When ([Work Tracking](../tickets-workflow/work-tracking.md#phases)).

## Why

A prototype answers its gate only when its questions are written before playing and answered after.

## Sections

- What It Tries - the core loop from the outline, in a limited environment, never the whole world.
- The Round - one full round of the loop, a step per bullet, as the player does it.
- The Loop Mechanism - the loop's primary mechanism by name, and what balances it. It is not necessarily timed: a wait against the time the player is away is one kind, and a turn-based battle balanced in its count of attacks is another. Being able to change the balance while playing finds the right one in a single sitting, never over several builds.
- What It Needs - the few systems the round cannot do without, each in its simplest version: movement, a way to carry things, a way to talk.
- What We Want To Learn - questions, each with its answer after playing. A question names the law it tests, so a no points at the law the loop breaks.
- What Comes Out Of It - the answer to the gate, a short list of what to change, and what the next phase decides first.
- Not In It - what the prototype cannot tell: the size of the world, the balance, the look, the story.

## The Questions

Per beat of the loop, the questions are written for the game: a group per step of the round.

For the whole loop, the questions are the same for every game:

- After one round, does the player want to start the next? ([Desire Before Reward](game-design-laws-progress.md#desire-before-reward))
- How long does one round take?
- Is there always something next to do, with a choice in it? ([Always Something To Do](game-design-laws-experience.md#always-something-to-do))
- Does the loop start small enough for each new thing to land? ([Adopt, Repeat, Confirm](game-design-laws-guidance.md#adopt-repeat-confirm-arc))
- Do the limits make the player feel a push and a pull? ([Earned Progress](game-design-laws-progress.md#earned-progress))
- Does the reward feel earned, and is there something to want with it? ([Earned Progress](game-design-laws-progress.md#earned-progress))
- Is everything done in the world, without a menu? ([Friction In The World](game-design-laws-world.md#friction-in-the-world))
- Do the basic controls feel good? ([Always Polished](game-design-laws-quality.md#always-polished))
- Does plain footage of it hold a viewer? ([Fun First](game-design-laws-experience.md#fun-first))

## How To Apply

- The prototype's one ticket takes its acceptance criteria from the round: a criterion per step that the director does while playing one round, and one that every question has an answer ([Prototype Mode Is Speed First](../behavior/prototype-mode-is-speed-first.md)).
- The page is a plan, and leaves when the phase is done. What comes out of it goes into the decided docs and the next phase's page.
