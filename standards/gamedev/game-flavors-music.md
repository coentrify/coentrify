# Game Flavors: Music

How music works in the game: what plays, when, and how it follows play. These are the seven main ones, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Music Mixes

- Music mixes by scope: area themes in towns with an adaptive score in fights, say: `- Music: Area Themes - the towns`, `- Music: Adaptive Score - the fights`.
- The laws hold over every flavor: a soft mix, varied repeats and a layer per scene ([Soft Sound](game-design-laws-experience.md#soft-sound)), one message across the senses ([In Harmony](game-design-laws-own-style.md#in-harmony), [Every Sense Carries It](game-design-laws-ui.md#every-sense-carries-it)), and a level that is measured, never guessed ([Measure Sound, Never Guess](measure-sound-never-guess.md)).
- Music that is always on stops being heard ([Habituation](../psychology/habituation.md), [Contrast](game-design-laws-world.md#contrast)).

## Adaptive Score

The music follows play, with layers rising and falling with the moment.

Example: a lone flute in the forest, joined by drums as the wolves draw near.

Weighs: music that matches the moment, and it is costly to compose and to build.

## Area Themes

Each place has a theme of its own.

Example: the harbor has its accordion, and the mines their bass drum.

Weighs: an identity for each place, so the player knows where they are by ear, and a loop repeats and can grate.

## Leitmotifs

Themes tied to characters or places return, and change with the story.

Example: the miller's tune plays slowly at his funeral, then brightly when his son reopens the mill.

Weighs: meaning that builds over the game, with an emotional payoff, and it has to be composed for the whole arc.

## Sparse

Mostly ambient sound, with music saved for the moments that matter.

Example: an hour of wind and footsteps, then strings as the valley opens up.

Weighs: the music lands hard when it comes, and long stretches can feel empty.

## Diegetic Only

Music comes only from the world itself: a busker, a jukebox, a radio.

Example: the only music in town comes from the busker by the fountain.

Weighs: grounded and immersive, and it can steer little emotion.

## Player's Choice

The player picks what plays, from stations or a playlist.

Example: the truck's radio has three stations, and the player switches between them.

Weighs: the player sets their own mood, and there is less authored atmosphere, and licensing costs.

## Rhythm

Play follows the beat.

Example: the blacksmith's hammer lands on the beat, and so must the player's.

Weighs: music and play become one, and it demands timing.
