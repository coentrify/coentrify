# Audience Profile Template

A game names who it is for in `docs/audience.md`: what binds its players, and how broad or niche that group is. The profile describes a group by what its people share, and is never a persona.

## Why

Who a game is for directs many choices later on, from the references it can make to how deep it goes.

## The Profile

- What binds them - the interest, the taste or the knowledge the players share, in a sentence or two.
- The reach - `broad`, `focused` or `niche`.
- What the reach means - a niche game goes deep, with specific references, and gives up width. A broad game goes wide, with references everyone gets.
- What they know - what the game can take for granted, and what it explains.
- What moves them and what bores them, as a group, each point with the finding it rests on where one exists ([Psychology](../psychology/overview.md)).
- The flavors it rules out or favors, each with its reason ([Game Flavors](game-flavors.md)).

## The Bound

- The profile serves the player, and never exploits a weakness of the group ([Game Design Laws: Values](game-design-laws-values.md)).

## How To Apply

- The Idea names the audience and its reach, and the profile grows from there ([Game Phases: Concept](game-phases-concept.md#idea)).
- The profile lives in the project. A finding about people that holds beyond the game is filed with coentrify ([File A Change With Coentrify](../behavior/file-a-change-with-coentrify.md)).
- A design choice cites the profile where it decides the choice, as part of what the project decided ([Creative Agency](../autonomy/creative-agency.md)).
