# Game Flavors: Story Structure

The shape of the story over the whole game: how it starts, builds and resolves. [Story Delivery](game-flavors-story-delivery.md) is how it is told, and The Secret and Reveal sit below it. These are the seven main shapes, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Shapes Mix

- Shapes mix by scope: a main structure, and the structure of its parts: `- Story Structure: Episodic - main`, `- Story Structure: Three Acts - each episode`.
- Every shape has a start and an end, an emergent story and a time loop included ([Start And End](game-design-laws-progress.md#start-and-end), [As Long As It Is Interesting](game-design-laws-progress.md#as-long-as-it-is-interesting)).

## Hero's Journey

Departure, trials and return, and the hero comes back changed.

Example: a lamplighter's apprentice leaves the village to relight the sun, and comes home changed.

Weighs: a familiar, satisfying arc, and a predictable one.

## Three Acts

Setup, confrontation and resolution.

Example: a heist that is planned, pulled off, then survived after it goes wrong.

Weighs: clear, rising tension, and it needs a conflict to drive it.

## Kishōtenketsu

Introduction, development, twist and reconciliation, with no conflict needed.

Example: a year in an orchard, planting and tending, a sudden frost, and the harvest that comes anyway.

Weighs: gentle, with no villain needed, and the twist has to carry it.

## Episodic

Self-contained episodes, loosely tied together.

Example: each week a different stranger checks into the motel with a story of their own.

Weighs: easy to enter and to pause, with natural breaks built in, and a weaker overall arc.

## Emergent

The game's systems write the story as it is played.

Example: a colony where every drought, feud and wedding comes from the simulation.

Weighs: a unique story every time, and little authored meaning.

## Time Loop

The same stretch of time repeats, and what the player learns is the progress.

Example: the festival day repeats until the player learns what goes wrong at midnight.

Weighs: knowledge is the progress, and the repetition can tire.

## Fragmented

Pieces found out of order, and the player puts them together.

Example: memories found out of order, pieced together by the player.

Weighs: the player builds the meaning, and it is easy to get lost.

## Sources

- Campbell, The Hero with a Thousand Faces, 1949, and Vogler, The Writer's Journey, 1992, for the Hero's Journey.
- Aristotle's Poetics, and Field, Screenplay, 1979, for the three acts.
- Kishōtenketsu, the classical Chinese and Japanese four-part form.
- Sylvester, Designing Games, 2013, on games as story generators.
- Public games, as references only: The Legend of Zelda for Hero's Journey, Telltale's series for Episodic, RimWorld for Emergent, Outer Wilds for Time Loop, Her Story for Fragmented.
