# Game Flavors: Morality

How the game judges what the player does. [Make Yourself](game-design-laws-story.md#make-yourself) asks the game not to steer the player's judgment, so a flavor that labels deeds says so. These are the six main ones, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## Karma Meter

One scale from good to evil, and the game reacts to the score. It labels deeds, so it sets that part of Make Yourself aside.

Example: helping the orphanage tips the needle toward light, and the shadow guild stops answering letters.

Weighs: clear feedback and a legible arc, and it flattens morals into points, a deed that is good or bad by perspective included, and players game it.

Conflicts: Reputation, Gray Morality, Layered Reputation, No Judgment and Written Morals.

## Reputation

Each group judges the player by what it saw, and the groups disagree.

Example: the dockworkers cheer the player for the strike, and the merchants double their prices.

Weighs: nuance and consequences per group, and it is complex to track.

Conflicts: Karma Meter and No Judgment.

## Gray Morality

No meter: people react to the deeds they know of, and the player can be a criminal and a good person at once.

Example: the player picks pockets in the market and pays the widow's rent with it, and the town loves them for both.

Weighs: true to life ([Recognizable With Tension](game-design-laws-story.md#recognizable-with-tension)), and no clear feedback, and it is harder to write.

Conflicts: Karma Meter, No Judgment and Written Morals.

## Layered Reputation

What the player did, who knows of it, and what each one makes of it, kept apart. A public standing everyone shares gives the legible arc, groups judge it by their own values, and insiders know what the public does not. The judging lives in the world's people, never in one scale.

Example: the town hails the player as its saint, the neighbor thinks them a fraud, and the one who saw the cellar knows the truth.

Weighs: a legible arc with the nuance of perspective, and the drama of the gap between face and truth, and it is the hardest to build.

Conflicts: Karma Meter and No Judgment.

## No Judgment

The game takes no moral stance, and deeds carry no moral reaction.

Example: the player can burn the farm or save it, and the world shrugs.

Weighs: full freedom, and deeds carry no moral weight.

Conflicts: Karma Meter, Reputation, Gray Morality, Layered Reputation and Written Morals.

## Written Morals

The hero's morals are written, and the player cannot act against them.

Example: the knight will not steal: the hand stops, even when the player tries.

Weighs: a coherent character, and the player's hand is held.

Conflicts: Karma Meter, Gray Morality and No Judgment.
