# Launch Strategy

A game picks how it goes out. The default is the quiet launch: the full game on day one, as a surprise.

## Why

A game meets its players at its best, the first time they hear of it.

## The Quiet Launch

- The full game is released on day one. No wishlist campaign comes before it, and no demo on the store page.
- The store page is set up before the release and stays unpublished, for internal review and testing.
- The one thing that travels is a video of the game being played: someone enjoying the main loop, or an interesting moment.
- The video goes out once the game can be bought. Its viewer reads the description, goes to the game and buys it.
- That video is no trailer: no cutscene, no voice-over and no targeted ad.
- No award campaigns, and no asking for attention.
- A vibe video or a demo for a collaborator is no marketing. A hired composer then knows what to base the music on.
- After the release, fixes and updates follow what the players do.

## The Other Choices

- A wishlist campaign: the store page goes up months early, and the release date is announced ahead.
- A public demo: cut from the game during Alpha, and shown at a festival before the release.
- A closed beta, in place of a public demo.
- Building in public: devlogs and a community from early on.
- A publisher: the marketing is in someone else's hands, for a share of the revenue.
- Early access: the game is sold before it is whole. What is sold is polished, and the rest is gated or left out, so it is a demo that is paid for.

## What To Weigh

- A good game sells itself.
- The quiet launch starts slower. A store shows a new game to more people when its wishlists and early sales are high.
- It leans on the game clipping well ([Fun First](game-design-laws-experience.md#fun-first)).
- It is for a small studio. A big studio launches another way.
- It carries [Respect](game-design-laws-experience.md#respect) to the time before the purchase.
- Issues show with a small crowd first, and are fixed before many players meet them.
- A wishlist spends the first moment a player hears of the game, and the second look has to convince more.
- A game wishlisted on its potential is checked at the release, and removed when the potential is not there. It takes a big game to come back from that.
- Every other choice spends attention before the game is done: the director's and the players'.
- A promise made early has to be kept. A date that slips, or a game that falls short of its trailer, costs trust.

## How To Apply

- The strategy is picked before Alpha, since a wishlist campaign or a festival demo starts months ahead.
- The pick stands on the page of the Release phase. A hygiene check fails that page without one.
