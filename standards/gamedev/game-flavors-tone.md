# Game Flavors: Tone

The emotional register a game speaks in: how it feels to be inside it. Tone stands at the top of the layers, and every layer below takes it. These are the nine main tones, never all there are: a game's own tone is an `own` pick, and more join the catalog as games find them. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Tones Mix

- Tones mix: a game picks a main tone, and accents where they fit, each naming its scope after a dash: `- Tone: Satirical - main`, `- Tone: Absurd - the world`.
- An accent works by contrast, a joke in a dark story or a sad ending to a comic one ([Contrast](game-design-laws-world.md#contrast), [Rules Broken On Purpose](game-design-laws-own-style.md#rules-broken-on-purpose)).
- The tone runs through the music, the picture and the story alike ([In Harmony](game-design-laws-own-style.md#in-harmony)), and the audience's reach sets how far it goes ([Audience Profile Template](audience-profile-template.md)).

## Comic

The game wants laughs, and nothing stays heavy for long.

Example: a heist where the vault is guarded by a very polite goose.

Weighs: it is easy to like, and hard to make matter.

## Earnest

Sincere and warm, it means what it says, with no wink.

Example: a lighthouse keeper writes letters to a daughter far away.

Weighs: it lands deep, and risks the sentimental.

## Dark

Heavy themes that land hard, with real stakes and discomfort on purpose, within the [Values](game-design-laws-values.md) laws.

Example: a town gives up one citizen a year for its harvest.

Weighs: it stays with the player, and narrows the audience.

## Black Comedy

Dark topics played for laughs, landing light.

Example: a funeral home in a price war with the one across the street.

Weighs: it can touch anything, and trivializes what it misjudges.

## Satirical

Humor aimed at power, institutions or society, exaggerating to expose them.

Example: a ministry of happiness fines anyone caught frowning.

Weighs: it is sharp and means something, and it dates fast and divides.

## Whimsical

Playful and gentle, led by wonder, with soft logic.

Example: a postman delivers letters between clouds.

Weighs: it invites wonder, and can feel slight.

## Melancholic

Bittersweet, a quiet sadness, beauty in loss.

Example: the last summer before a village is flooded for a dam.

Weighs: it lingers long, at a slow pace.

## Absurd

The world runs on nonsense, played straight.

Example: in a ministry, every form is stamped by a frog, and nobody asks why.

Weighs: it is original, and loses the player unless its nonsense keeps its own rules ([Fair Surprise](game-design-laws-world.md#fair-surprise)).

## Cozy

Comfort and safety, low stakes, and nothing really goes wrong.

Example: tending a tea garden as the seasons pass.

Weighs: it relaxes, with little tension.
