# Game Flavors: Together

Who the player plays with. Much of [Game Design Laws: Companions](game-design-laws-companions.md) holds for other players too. These are the seven main ones, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Playing Together Mixes

- It mixes by scope: a solo story with co-op side content, say: `- Together: Solo - the story`, `- Together: Co-op - the side quests`.
- One player's pause cannot stop everyone's world online, so a game that plays live with others sets [Easy Pause](game-design-laws-experience.md#easy-pause) and [AFK Safe](game-design-laws-experience.md#afk-safe) aside where they cannot hold, with its reason.
- An experience shared is felt more strongly ([Shared Experience](../psychology/shared-experience.md)).

## Solo

One player, alone with the game.

Example: a long winter in a mountain cabin, played alone.

Weighs: the player controls the pace and the story, and there are no memories shared with anyone.

## Co-op

Players work together toward one goal.

Example: two players carry one piano up a winding stair.

Weighs: triumphs that are shared, and the slowest player sets the pace.

## Competitive

Players play against each other. It stands outside [No Try Hard](game-design-laws-progress.md#no-try-hard).

Example: four rival bakers race to fill the king's order first.

Weighs: high stakes and mastery, and toxicity, and casual players are shut out.

## Asymmetric

Players take on different roles, such as one against many.

Example: one player is the ghost, and the others are the house's tenants.

Weighs: fresh dynamics every match, and roles that differ are hard to balance.

## Same Couch

Players share one screen in one room.

Example: two players on one sofa, one steering the cart, one throwing the bombs.

Weighs: the warmest way to play together, laughing in one room, and it needs one screen and one place.

## Shared World

Many players in one persistent world.

Example: a whole server rebuilds the burned market together.

Weighs: a living society, and it is costly to run.

## Asynchronous Traces

Players never meet, and leave traces for each other.

Example: a rope another player tied on the cliff stays there for everyone after.

Weighs: company without pressure, and traces can be abused, so they need moderation.
