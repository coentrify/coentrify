# Game Flavors: Power

How the player grows stronger over the game. The player starts with as little as possible and earns the rest ([Earned Progress](game-design-laws-progress.md#earned-progress)). These are the seven main kinds of power, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Power Mixes

- Kinds of power mix by scope: stats with gear, or an empire with allies: `- Power: Stats - the hero`, `- Power: Gear - the hero`, `- Power: Empire - the business`.
- The laws hold over every kind: the player wants a thing before having it ([Desire Before Reward](game-design-laws-progress.md#desire-before-reward)), and the rewards grow in a groove ([Cadence](game-design-laws-progress.md#cadence)).

## Skill Only

The character never changes, and the player gets better.

Example: the sword never gets sharper, and the player's timing does.

Weighs: pure mastery and fairness, and it is try-hard by nature, so a casual player rarely enjoys it fully. Competitive games live here.

## Stats

The numbers grow: levels, attributes, a skill tree.

Example: each level adds a point to strength, patience or charm.

Weighs: steady growth, and a fallback for a less skilled player, who can level past a wall, and numbers can replace play and invite grinding.

## Gear

Power comes from what the player finds or makes.

Example: the rusty spade becomes a silver one, and digs through clay.

Weighs: it rewards finding and making, and the inventory can become a chore.

## Abilities

New moves open up new actions.

Example: learning to glide opens up the cliffs the player could only look at before.

Weighs: every new move changes the play itself, which suits an [Interconnected](game-flavors-world-structure.md#interconnected) world, and each one is costly to design well.

## Allies

The player's strength is the people who join them ([Game Design Laws: Companions](game-design-laws-companions.md)).

Example: the ferryman, the forger and the cook join, and each opens a new way through.

Weighs: power lives in relationships, and an ally has to pull their weight without taking over ([Helps, Never Takes Over](game-design-laws-companions.md#helps-never-takes-over)).

## Empire

Strength is what the player owns and runs: a business, a territory, a network.

Example: one market stall becomes a market, and the market moves the city's prices.

Weighs: growth the player sees in the world, a satisfying climb, and managing it can bury the play.

## Meta Progression

Power carries over between runs or deaths.

Example: each failed descent leaves a coin for the shrine, which grows stronger.

Weighs: failure still pays, and the player can grind past a challenge instead of mastering it.

## Sources

- Koster, A Theory of Fun for Game Design, 2004, on mastery as the heart of fun.
- Public games, as references only: Celeste for Skill Only, Pokémon and Diablo for Stats and Gear, Metroid for Abilities, Suikoden for Allies, tycoon and city games for Empire, Hades for Meta Progression.
