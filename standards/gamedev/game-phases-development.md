# Game Phases: Development

The third stage of a game ([Game Phases](game-phases.md)). The designed game is built.

## Pre-Alpha

Functionally complete: the full game playable from start to finish, perhaps badly paced, buggy and visually imperfect, with every part there. Gate: is every part there?

- Every part a level needs is present and reads as what it is: its objects, sounds, voices, music or its silence, and the interface it needs.
- A rough part is fine. A missing part is not.
- The right vibe counts.
- Polish waits: the look and the finish of a working part belong to Alpha, and so does its debt, until the game's whole shape stands. An agent pushes back on polish.
- What a part does is good from its first version ([Always Polished](game-design-laws-quality.md#always-polished)).
- Content the director has not decided takes the agent's recommendation under Veto ([Game Work Workflow](game-work-workflow.md#content)), and Alpha polishes it.
- A ticket names the parts it adds in refinement, by kind, so a story shows what it brings: ten sound effects, say.
- Before a level lands, an inventory checks every part its tickets and its feature doc name: each is present and reads as what it is.
- The [Launch Strategy](launch-strategy.md) is picked before Alpha.

## Alpha

Completeness: everything implemented properly everywhere and looking good, its passes done. A pass is an epic of checks and fine-tuning across every level, never new mechanics, so it belongs to Alpha. Gate: is every part right?

## Beta

External, controlled playtesters. Gate: do strangers get it and keep playing?
