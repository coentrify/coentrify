# Game Flavors: Choice

How much the player's decisions shape the story. What the player decides lasts ([Lasting Impact](game-design-laws-progress.md#lasting-impact)), and a flavor that sets that aside says so. These are the four main ones, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Choices Mix

- Choices mix by scope: a linear main story with branching side stories, say: `- Choice: Linear Story - main`, `- Choice: Branching - the side stories`.
- A choice is worth making when it is an interesting decision, with no answer that is plainly right.

## Linear Story

The story walks one road, and the player's choices are in how they play.

Example: how the player fights and builds is theirs, and the story still walks one road.

Weighs: a tight, authored story, and the player's say lives only in play.

## Branching

Decisions split the story into paths. A line of choices the player keeps, out of conviction, is honored to the end, even when the player has lost hope in it.

Example: siding with the miners or the owners splits the second half.

Weighs: choices with real weight, and it is costly, and fewer players see each path.

## Lasting Choices

No big branches, and small choices leave marks that stay ([Shared Memories](game-design-laws-companions.md#shared-memories)).

Example: the stray dog the player fed in the first hour turns up at the final battle.

Weighs: small choices felt for a long time, and the game has to remember everything.

## Illusion Of Choice

Choices that feel meaningful and come back together. Sometimes a story has to converge, and the choice then lies in how, never whether. It sets Lasting Impact aside, so a game that picks it leaves the law out with its reason.

Example: whatever the player answers, the captain sails at dawn.

Weighs: the feel of agency at low cost, and once noticed it cheapens every choice.

## Sources

- Sid Meier, Interesting Decisions, GDC 2012, on a game as a series of interesting decisions.
- Public games, as references only: The Last of Us for Linear Story, Detroit: Become Human for Branching, Telltale's series for the critique of Illusion Of Choice.
