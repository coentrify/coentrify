# Game Flavors: Time

How time runs in the game, and who controls it. These are the six main ones, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Time Mixes

- Time mixes by scope: real-time exploring with turn-based battles is the classic pairing: `- Time: Real Time - exploring`, `- Time: Turn Based - battles`.
- The laws hold over every flavor: pausing is always easy ([Easy Pause](game-design-laws-experience.md#easy-pause)), a clock may stop at the day's end until the player ends the day ([Natural Breaks](game-design-laws-experience.md#natural-breaks)), and its pressure is weighed ([Compulsion Is Considered](game-design-laws-values.md#compulsion-is-considered)).
- Nothing is lost while the player is away ([AFK Safe](game-design-laws-experience.md#afk-safe)), with a pause button or a world that waits. What the world does while the game is closed is [Away Time](game-flavors-away-time.md).

## Real Time

Time runs on, and the player acts within it.

Example: the storm rolls in whether the player is ready or not.

Weighs: tension and immediacy, and little time to think, so it leans on reflexes.

## Turn Based

Nothing happens until the player acts, and the world waits.

Example: the dragon waits, claws raised, until the player has made a move.

Weighs: room to think and plan, and less tension and a slower pace.

## Pausable Real Time

Real time that the player can stop to plan.

Example: the sea battle freezes at a key press while the player aims each cannon.

Weighs: the energy of real time with room to plan, and pausing can break the flow.

## The Clock

A day or a calendar runs and drives the game.

Example: the bakery opens at six, and the bread has to be ready.

Weighs: urgency and a rhythm of days, and its pressure can push the player.

## Bendable Time

The player rewinds, slows or speeds up time.

Example: a fall into the river is undone with a turn of the pocket watch.

Weighs: mistakes undone and experiments encouraged ([Mistakes](game-flavors-mistakes.md)), and the stakes drop.

## Real World Time

The game follows the real clock and calendar.

Example: it is snowing in the game because it is December outside.

Weighs: the game lives alongside the player's life and invites daily visits, and it waits on the calendar, and skipping time tempts.

## Sources

- Public games, as references only: Civilization for Turn Based, Baldur's Gate for Pausable Real Time, Stardew Valley for The Clock, Braid for Bendable Time, Animal Crossing for Real World Time.
