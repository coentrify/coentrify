# Game Flavors: Difficulty

How hard the game is, and who sets it. Success is a window, never an exact point ([Bands, Not Bullseyes](game-design-laws-progress.md#bands-not-bullseyes)), and a game made for every type of player never requires playing try hard ([No Try Hard](game-design-laws-progress.md#no-try-hard)). These are the five main ones, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## How Difficulty Mixes

- Difficulty mixes by scope: Fixed with Assist Options, or Chosen with Opt-In Challenge.
- A game made for every type of player that picks Fixed softens it with Assist Options.
- The aim is the channel between boredom and anxiety ([Flow](../psychology/flow.md), [Optimal Arousal](../psychology/optimal-arousal.md)).

## Fixed

One difficulty, the same for everyone.

Example: the frost giant hits just as hard for everyone, and every player meets the same fight.

Weighs: a shared experience everyone can talk about, and it shuts some players out.

## Chosen

The player picks a level, at the start or at any time.

Example: at the gate, the player picks whether the guards are sleepy, alert or paranoid.

Weighs: the player decides, and before knowing the game, and pride keeps some on a level that is too hard.

## Adaptive

The game quietly tunes itself to the player.

Example: after three falls at the same gap, the wind quietly eases off.

Weighs: smooth flow for everyone, and it feels like cheating once noticed, and mastery feels less earned.

## Assist Options

One designed difficulty, with tools that soften parts of it.

Example: a slider slows the game to half speed, and the jumps stay the same.

Weighs: one design, open to more players, and some feel it cheapens the challenge.

## Opt-In Challenge

The player takes on harder rules for greater rewards.

Example: each candle lit at the shrine makes the dungeon meaner and the gold brighter.

Weighs: depth and long replay for those who want it, and the base game has to stand on its own.

## Sources

- Hunicke, The Case for Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment in Games, ACE 2005, for Adaptive.
- The Flow and Optimal Arousal findings, for the channel between boredom and anxiety.
- Public games, as references only: Dark Souls for Fixed, Resident Evil 4 for Adaptive, Celeste for Assist Options, Hades for Opt-In Challenge.
