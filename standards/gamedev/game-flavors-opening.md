# Game Flavors: Opening

How the game begins: the first minutes, and how the player steps into the story. These are the six main openings, never all there are. The index and the way a game uses its flavors are in [Game Flavors](game-flavors.md).

## The First Minutes

- One opening leads, and a Cold Open or a Taste Of Power can come before any other but Straight Into Play.
- The laws on the first minutes hold over every opening: they are fun ([Fun First](game-design-laws-experience.md#fun-first)), they hold no dumb tutorial ([Respect](game-design-laws-experience.md#respect)), and one new thing comes at a time ([Adopt, Repeat, Confirm](game-design-laws-guidance.md#adopt-repeat-confirm-arc)).

## In Medias Res

The player starts in the middle of something.

Example: the player wakes on a runaway train, with a stranger shouting their name.

Weighs: an instant hook and momentum, and the player lacks context, so the stakes have to explain themselves.

Conflicts: Ordinary World, Prologue and Straight Into Play.

## Ordinary World

The player starts in everyday life, before anything happens.

Example: a Monday in a fishing town, with nets to mend, a mother to visit, and a letter that has not come.

Weighs: it grounds the player and makes the later change felt, and it is a slow start.

Conflicts: In Medias Res, Prologue and Straight Into Play.

## Cold Open

A gripping scene first, often from later or elsewhere, and then the story starts.

Example: a palace burns at night, and then: ten days earlier.

Weighs: a promise and a hook, and it can overwhelm before the controls are learned.

Conflicts: Taste Of Power and Straight Into Play.

## Prologue

A short, playable piece of the past that sets the stakes.

Example: the player sails the grandmother's last voyage before taking over as her grandson.

Weighs: stakes and history, and it delays the real game.

Conflicts: In Medias Res, Ordinary World and Straight Into Play.

## Taste Of Power

The player starts with a setup from later in the game, plays it briefly, and loses it, so the climb back starts with the wanting in place ([Desire Before Reward](game-design-laws-progress.md#desire-before-reward)). The taste comes from the early middle, never the peak. It shows what the game can become and guides toward a setup, and enough lies beyond it to find unguided.

Example: a factory game opens on a humming line that smelts ore and stamps gears, until a storm leaves the player one drill and a pickaxe.

Weighs: the player knows where the climb leads and wants it. A loss in the first minutes can feel like a cheat, so the game takes it for a reason, never as the player's failure.

Conflicts: Cold Open and Straight Into Play.

## Straight Into Play

No story at all, playing from the first second.

Example: the ball is already rolling when the screen fades in.

Weighs: fun at once, and no story hook.

Conflicts: In Medias Res, Ordinary World, Cold Open, Prologue and Taste Of Power.

## Sources

- Horace, Ars Poetica, for in medias res.
- Campbell's ordinary world, from the Hero's Journey.
- The cold open, from television.
- Public games, as references only: Uncharted 2 for In Medias Res, Half-Life for the tram ride into an ordinary day, Red Dead Redemption 2 for Prologue, Castlevania: Symphony of the Night and Metroid Prime for Taste Of Power, Pac-Man for Straight Into Play.
